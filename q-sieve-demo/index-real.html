A working demo. A bounded claim.

Q-SIEVE is a trust layer for already-flagged account windows. It consolidates behavior, compares windows using a quantum fidelity kernel, and routes outcomes through an explicit decision policy.

  • All accounts, payments and labels are from the public IBM AML HI-Small dataset. The 12 displayed windows were selected across the score range from a separate 3,000-window test pool and are illustrative, not a measure of overall reliability.
  • The 12-qubit angle feature map is evaluated with an exact statevector simulator for the benchmark, and the frozen 6-qubit kernel was executed on three real QPUs (IBM marrakesh, IQM Garnet, VTT Q50). Scores and nearest training examples are precomputed; the browser runs policy and workflow logic. Auto-clear defaults off because the held-out test leaves 27 labelled-suspicious among 425 below-cutoff windows.
  • A score is a model decision signal, not a calibrated probability or a causal explanation. Behavior cards show observed inputs; precedent similarity is not evidence of guilt.
  • Labels are the dataset’s own. Neither this evaluation nor the demo thresholds demonstrate production safety, quantum advantage, or label efficiency.
  • Audit entries append within this local browser session. Local storage is editable and resettable; this is not a tamper-proof, access-controlled compliance log.
  • Production validation needs independent data, calibration, bias assessment, temporal testing, security and governance. Priority and investigation-held alerts remain open for a human.
